Structure

Drag across a wall in the canvas to trace it. Finishing a segment leaves its line as a snap guide — start the next wall anywhere near that line, even with a gap, and it lands perfectly in line. Fine-tune an individual wall's thickness afterward in the panel below.

Doors come in several types (above) — drag any of them onto a wall the same way, then drag the small end handles of a selected door to stretch it to any width (sliding and bifold doors add panels as they grow). Change a placed door's type in the panel below.

Press and drag either button onto a wall to drop it there — a door cuts an opening in the wall; a window sits on the wall and takes its thickness. Drop away from a wall to cancel. Once placed: drag a door across the wall to flip which side it swings into, or along the wall past its own width to flip which end it's hinged from (or use the buttons below). Drag a window's end handle to extend it along the wall, or its amber centre handle to bow it out into a bay/bow window (an even arc between its two ends).

Auto-detect (pixel-based)

No AI, no cost, fully deterministic — traces actual solid black lines in the image rather than guessing coordinates. Pick a reference wall first (use the THINNEST wall you care about, usually an interior partition — exterior walls are often drawn thicker, and a threshold set from those alone misses thinner ones).

Once the walls above look right, this scans along them for windows — no separate calibration needed, and every result sits exactly on a confirmed wall's line.

Recommended over the AI door detect below: matches the exact arc+leaf shape a door symbol draws against each wall's own line — deterministic, so re-running gives the same result every time (unlike the AI version, which read the same image differently call to call with no way to fix that).

Detection only ever adds shapes — re-running without clearing first will duplicate whatever's already there. Use the clear buttons above for a clean retest.

Trace from the image

Claude reads the plan — every room, its printed size, the doors, windows, stairs and furniture — and the walls are then measured exactly from the drawing itself. Takes about a minute, a few pence on your Anthropic account. One floor per image (use Split, below, on a listing picture showing several).

Older detect tools

If this floor's image still shows more than one level (e.g. a listing image with Ground/First/Cellar together), run Split first — the two buttons below detect only within whatever image is on this tab, and can't tell your intended floor apart from another one shown in the same picture.

Door-only alternative to the pixel-based detector above: reads each door's rough position plus hinge/swing off the printed arc symbol, then snaps that onto the nearest confirmed wall. Kept for comparison, but found to be inconsistent in practice — re-running on an unchanged image gave different placement/swing each time, even on Opus 5, with no setting available to fix that (temperature is rejected outright on Opus 5/Sonnet 5). Try the pixel-based detector first.

Rugs

Pick a colour, then a shape. A rug always sits underneath the furniture. Select a rug and pick a colour to recolour it.

Shades the ideal clearance off dining chairs/tables, sofas, beds, toilets, sinks and kitchen appliances (UK-typical: NKBA / Approved Document M / trade press) — a guide, not a pass/fail check. Not covered: hallway width (min. 90cm), kitchen aisle between facing runs (~100–110cm one cook, 120–135cm two).
